# Preface

We find ourselves at an unbelievable moment in time. 

We now possess a technology which, without exaggeration, closely matches, if not outright surpasses, our own intelligence.[^1] A technology which, in a short amount of time, has gone from barely being able to write coherent sentences to being capable of solving problems at the edge of human understanding. 

Such a technology raises questions that, increasingly, make us wonder about the true nature of thought, consciousness, and even our very existence. These weighty questions are difficult to comprehend; philosophers have been contemplating them for thousands of years. While significant, however, there’s another set of questions, about work, that is likely more pressing for the average human. Answering them might even help us shed some light on the questions about the self. 

Among them: What does the future of work look like, in an era where reasoning alone no longer suffices to separate man from machine? What does it even mean to work?

These are a set of questions that have been buzzing inside my head like an angry swarm of bees, to the point where I found myself forced to spill them onto paper. I don’t mean to come across as pretentious or even attention-seeking. On the contrary, I think many people are having the same thoughts. In some sense, these questions are very basic and I approach them with little formal training and even less formal research. I’m really just journaling, although I can’t shake the feeling that these journal entries might be better understood as a small contribution to a larger zeitgeist.

I have created an open source project, CoCheck, which builds off the concepts I developed as I wrote these essays. But I didn’t write these essays for CoCheck. They’re closer to shower thoughts: I wrote them because I can’t stop thinking about this stuff. My only real takeaway? I think the best thing we can do when faced with a new and potentially terrifying technology like AI is to understand it: learn it, experiment with it, and safely spread that understanding as widely as possible, so that everyone can opine on it. I believe by doing so, we can improve our collective fortunes. AI is here to stay, no matter how opposed we are to it. We may as well find our way through it. 

[^1]:  In an increasing number of situations. It is, however, still quite bad at many things.
